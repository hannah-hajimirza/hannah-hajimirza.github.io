Carbon Pricing and the Reallocation of Bank Credit
Working paper · 2025

I examine how banks adjust lending to firms affected by carbon pricing. Using global syndicated loan data around the introduction of 26 carbon pricing policies, I document three findings. First, banks with greater pre-existing exposure to covered borrowers reduce these borrowers' share of new lending by 2.14 percentage points after implementation, about one-third of its initial level. The decline begins before the carbon price applies and holds within the same firm and the same loan. Second, these banks move new lending toward uncovered firms based outside the covered borrowers' home countries. Covered firms' other existing lenders increase new lending to them, and their total syndicated borrowing does not clearly fall. Third, their new lending becomes more concentrated across industries, and they share fewer borrowers with each other, with no measurable change in bank risk. These findings show how existing bank portfolios shape both the timing and direction of lending responses to carbon pricing.

Biodiversity risks and lender credit allocation: The role of lenders’ commitments to environmental sustainability
With Hossein Asgharian, Sara Jonsson, and Lu Liu · SSRN · November 2025

This paper examines whether voluntary sustainability commitments are associated with differences in lenders’ responses to biodiversity risks in credit allocation. Using global syndicated loan data matched with firm-level measures of biodiversity impact and dependency, we find that lenders committed to the Principles for Responsible Banking (PRB) extend smaller loans to firms with greater biodiversity impacts than non-committed lenders. In contrast, we find no significant difference between PRB-committed and non-committed lenders in their lending response to borrowers’ biodiversity dependency. The differential response to biodiversity impact extends beyond GHG-related pressures and remains significant after accounting for climate risk and pre-existing lender characteristics, including historical exposure to environmentally sensitive industries. We further examine selection into PRB membership, finding that initial adoption is associated with bank size and international presence, while subsequent adoption is related to prior adoption among co-syndication partners. Overall, our findings suggest that sustainability commitments are associated with greater sensitivity to borrowers’ biodiversity impacts in credit allocation decisions.
